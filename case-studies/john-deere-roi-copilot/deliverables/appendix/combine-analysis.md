# One combine, two crops: the ownership decision remains indeterminate

**Iowa · 2,400 total acres · 1,200 corn / 1,200 soybean · evidence reviewed September 22, 2026**

Sharing one base combine corrects the utilization picture, but does not establish a purchase case. The curated operation capacities produce **134.6939 corn hours + 88.3929 soybean hours = 223.0867 shared-base hours/year** (223.1 displayed). Base capital and operating hours count once; the corn head and draper remain separate assets. At the same 50/50 mix, shared use is 46.4764 hours at 500 acres and 929.5281 hours at 10,000 acres. The [shared-use exhibit](../figures/combine-shared-use.png) shows why either crop alone understates base utilization. Crossing the author-selected 200-hour line establishes neither monetary break-even nor harvest feasibility.

The only supported annual price comparison input is a dated survey service baseline. The [March 2026 Iowa State A3-10 survey](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/pdf/a3-10.pdf) gives generic combine-only averages of $46.05/corn acre and $43.05/soybean acre: **$106,920/year** for this farm. Separately, substituting its $48.20/acre soybean-with-draper service rate gives **$113,100/year**. That sensitivity buys a harvesting service; it is not head rent. Rates include fuel and operator labor. Both displays exclude grain cart, hauling/trucking, and drying and are nominal 2026 USD, not local quotes or scheduling commitments.

**New and used ownership totals, cost gaps, monetary break-even acreage, and recommended acreage/crop-mix ranges are indeterminate.** No genuine new three-asset price or sufficiently documented used configuration is available. Public asks in [sources §9](sources.md#9-combine-ownership-custom-hire-and-timing-evidence--research-gate) are unqualified market observations, not economic scenarios; incomplete item links and condition evidence preclude package totals. The correction supports evaluating shared ownership, but supports no purchase or hire recommendation yet. Acquisition value, capital rate, repairs, terminal resale, crop mix, fuel/labor, and custom rates could change the ranking; credible bounds are needed before calling any choice robust or sensitive.

Cost and timing must both pass. Corn and soybean need separate dated windows, with overlapping shared-machine hours allocated once. [ISU A3-25](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/AGDm/crops/pdf/a3-25.pdf) supplies historical suitable-day medians, not guaranteed harvest hours; [NASS's 2024 Iowa review](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Statistics_by_State/Iowa/Publications/Crop_Progress_%26_Condition/2024/IA-Annual-Crop-Progress-2024.pdf) documents overlapping crop harvests. Farm windows, shifts, head changes, outages, and grain-handling capacity are missing, so owned timing is **unknown**. Hire timing is also **unknown** without a written commitment covering both crops, consistent with [ISU A3-33](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/crops/pdf/a3-33.pdf). The 14-day exhibit cut is not a validated deadline. Neither the draft segment design nor legacy landscape agreement (50%, 50%, 25% at 500, 2,400, 10,000 acres) validates this recommendation.

## Methods and evidence required to resolve the decision

| Input / method | Current basis and concrete evidence checklist |
|---|---|
| Workload | One pass per crop; use unrounded capacities 8.909090909090908 corn and 13.575757575757576 soybean acres/hour from [operations](../../data/reference/operations.json). Confirm farm-specific achievable capacity. |
| New and used acquisition | Obtain dated, itemized base + corn-head + draper quotes with exact models/serials, adapters, freight, warranty, taxes, and price basis. For used assets, capture starting age, hours/acres, inspection and repair history, and exact compatibility; reconcile engine versus separator hours. |
| Annual economic cost | Use one common holding horizon, discount rate, currency basis, and service scope. Annualize purchase less discounted terminal resale plus discounted operating charges. Include each asset once; add incremental repairs, fuel/lube, labor, taxes, insurance, storage, and matched services. Do not add loan principal, interest, or depreciation again. Obtain documented values/ranges for every missing component. |
| Repairs and resale | [A3-29 parsed classes](../../data/reference/isu_a3_29_machine_data.json) distinguish base and heads. Apply repairs as accumulated cost at ending use less accumulated cost at starting use; retain actual used wear. Obtain separate repair/list-price bases and terminal resale bounds for all three assets. Generic head coefficients do not replace condition evidence. |
| Farm timing | Record district, crop start/finish dates, crew shifts, Sunday policy, grain handling, observed changeover time, and machine-specific outage evidence. Apply suitable days × hours/day minus losses, allocating overlap once. Weather medians are not probability bounds. |
| Hire contract | Obtain a written crop-specific proposal stating acres/fields, heads including draper, rates and fuel/operator treatment, cart/haul/drying scope, dates or priority, completion commitment, weather exceptions, cancellation, and substitute-machine terms. Match owner service scope. |
| Decision ranges | After gaps close, vary acreage, crop mix, price, capital rate, repairs/resale, fuel/labor, hire rates, workable days, and downtime over documented or explicitly exploratory bounds. Report a monetary equality only within a tested bracket at fixed crop mix/parameters; show ties, reversals, unknown timing, and infeasibility. Require a supported cost advantage and feasible timing before assigning a winner. |

Detailed boundaries: [assumptions §12](assumptions.md#12-dual-use-combine-research-gate). Missing values remain unknown, never zero; no local quote or contractor outreach is represented here.
